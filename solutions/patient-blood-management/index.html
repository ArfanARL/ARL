HEALTHCARE PLATFORMS

Patient Blood Management, from vein to vein.

Blood safety built into our AI-enabled healthcare information system: donor to bedside traceability, verification at every handover, monitored transfusions and haemovigilance.

Part of the Provincial HMISFuture global impact

WHY IT MATTERS

Blood is a scarce, living resource.

Patient Blood Management is a patient-centred approach: care for the patient’s own blood, avoid transfusion where it is not needed, and make it safe where it is. Paper records and disconnected systems make each of these harder, especially at handovers between blood bank, ward and bedside.

Our approach connects the blood bank with the wards and the longitudinal patient record, so information follows both the patient and the unit.

01

Protect the patient's own blood

Anaemia is easier to manage when it is seen early. Haemoglobin and other results, diagnoses, medicines and planned surgery sit in one patient record, and the surgery briefing includes anaemia.

02

Limit avoidable blood loss and transfusion

Clinicians can review transfusion history and the clinical reason for a request before blood is issued. Each request is raised as an order inside the hospital system.

03

Make every transfusion safe

When blood is needed, the unit and the patient are verified, the transfusion is monitored, and any reaction is recorded and followed up.

VEIN-TO-VEIN TRACEABILITY

One connected chain.

  1. 01

    Donor

    Registration, screening questionnaire aligned with AABB and FDA practice, 90-day donation interval and donor card.

  2. 02

    Collection

    Each donation receives an identification number and an ISBT 128 four-quadrant label.

  3. 03

    Testing

    Screening for HIV, hepatitis B, hepatitis C, syphilis and malaria; two-sample forward and reverse ABO grouping; reagent quality control.

  4. 04

    Inventory

    Quarantine until results are cleared, temperature logging and controlled discard.

  5. 05

    Issue

    Crossmatch with antiglobulin phases, informed by the patient's group, antibodies, allergies and transfusion history. A crossmatch stays valid for 72 hours.

  6. 06

    Receiving

    Receipt of the unit on the ward is recorded, so responsibility is clear at each handover.

  7. 07

    Bedside

    Five-rights check before the transfusion starts, with vital signs before, during and after.

  8. 08

    Recall and lookback

    Units can be recalled, and donors and recipients traced when a transfusion-transmitted infection is suspected.

CAPABILITIES

Safety at every step.

IDENTIFICATION

Right patient, right unit

  • Barcode checks from blood bank to bedside to prevent mismatched transfusions
  • Patient wristband scanning for positive identification
  • Hands-free verification on a smartphone
  • Cross-checks of patient, unit, blood group and order
  • Point-of-care blood group check at the bedside
  • Visual inspection of the blood bag recorded before use
TRACKING

Know where every unit is

  • Real-time status and location of each blood unit
  • Receipt from blood bank to ward, with an alert when receipt is delayed
  • Pauses in the transfusion timeline are recorded
  • Unused units returned to the blood bank, or kept allocated to the patient
  • Access controlled by user role and location
  • Turnaround time from request to transfusion
BEDSIDE

Manage the transfusion

  • A clear timeline for each transfusion
  • Several transfusions handled for one patient, including simultaneous ones
  • Pause and resume with the event recorded
  • Vital-sign stages configurable to the hospital's procedure
  • Default observations before, at 15, 30 and 60 minutes, and after
  • Transfusion history available in the patient file
ALERTS

Act before harm occurs

  • Blood not transfused within the allowed time
  • Point-of-care group not verified
  • Wrong patient, or a transfusion time limit exceeded
  • A suspected transfusion reaction
  • More than four red cell units within one hour, prompting massive transfusion review
  • A massive transfusion protocol workflow
HAEMOVIGILANCE

Respond to reactions

  • Date, time and type of reaction, using AABB-aligned categories
  • Decision to stop or continue the transfusion
  • Volume transfused when the reaction occurred
  • Items returned to the blood bank for investigation
  • Investigation results verified by a haematologist
  • Reactions kept with the patient record for future crossmatches
REPORTING

Evidence for review

  • End-to-end traceability report with filters
  • Turnaround-time reporting
  • Staff activity and productivity records
  • Donor-to-recipient history for audit and recall
  • Stock, discard and temperature records
  • Data to support hospital transfusion committees

The system supports hospital procedures and professional judgement; it does not replace them. Features are configured and validated for each deployment.

FUTURE GLOBAL IMPACT

Where this can make a difference, especially in Europe and the USA.

EUROPE

Traceability and vigilance

The EU Regulation on substances of human origin (2024/1938), which includes blood, applies from 7 August 2027 and replaces the earlier Blood Directive. It places weight on donor and recipient protection, traceability and vigilance. A digital donor-to-bedside record with structured reaction reporting could help hospitals and blood establishments prepare that evidence consistently, alongside national requirements and data-protection law.

UNITED STATES

Safety programmes and certification

Hospitals accredited by The Joint Commission can seek its voluntary Patient Blood Management certification, offered with AABB. Verified identification, monitored transfusions, reaction follow-up and traceability reports are the kind of day-to-day records such programmes rely on. The FDA regulates blood establishment computer software as a medical device, so any use within that scope would need the appropriate clearance first.

GLOBAL

Access where resources are limited

WHO estimates that impaired blood health affects more than three billion people, and in 2025 it published guidance on implementing Patient Blood Management. A platform designed for provincial scale, able to run on existing computers and smartphones, could help health systems adopt safer transfusion practice without a large new hardware investment.

Expected benefits

  1. 01
    Fewer identification errors

    Scanning and two-step checks at the bedside are designed to stop a mismatch before the transfusion begins.

  2. 02
    Less wasted blood

    Visibility of each unit, receipt alerts and a return route can reduce units lost to delay or poor handling.

  3. 03
    Faster response

    Time-based and reaction alerts bring problems to staff attention while action is still possible.

  4. 04
    Better national data

    Structured traceability and reaction records can support haemovigilance, planning of donor supply and research.

  5. 05
    Responsible use of AI

    Future work includes blood demand forecasting, anaemia risk flagging before planned surgery and wastage analysis. These would be validated before use, with clinicians accountable for decisions.

This section describes potential, not measured outcomes. Agentic Research Labs does not claim CE marking, FDA clearance or accreditation for this system. Deployment in the European Union, the United States or elsewhere would follow the applicable approvals, data-protection law and local clinical validation.
